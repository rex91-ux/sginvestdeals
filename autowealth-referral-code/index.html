The CPF OA portfolio in full

AutoWealth’s CPF portfolios invest Ordinary Account savings from S$3,000 for a flat 0.28% a year advisory fee; brokerage costs are absorbed. Unit-trust expense ratios of 0.32% (Risk 2) to 0.40% (Risk 5) are charged by the fund managers, after AutoWealth rebates 100% of trailer fees. Your CPF agent bank charges about S$2–S$2.50 per deposit or withdrawal plus S$2–S$2.50 a quarter. Holdings sit in a segregated account in your name at Phillip Securities.

How much is the AutoWealth referral reward?

AutoWealth’s referral message promises a S$20 top-up to both your account and the referrer’s when you sign up with the code 20IOUG-referral. AutoWealth hasn’t published the referral conditions on its website, so check in the app what you need to deposit and when the top-up is paid.

How much does AutoWealth charge?

AutoWealth Starter charges 0.5% a year plus a flat platform fee of US$18 per account, with transaction, custody and rebalancing costs absorbed by AutoWealth — about S$74 a year on S$10,000 by AutoWealth’s own example. CPF OA portfolios cost 0.28% a year, with fund expense ratios of 0.32%–0.40% and CPF agent-bank charges on top. Flexi Cash is 0.1%, and Plus+ charges an 8% performance fee on profits instead.

Is AutoWealth cheaper than Endowus, Syfe or StashAway?

From about S$15,000 to S$250,000, yes: S$524 a year on S$100,000 against S$600 at Syfe, S$654 at Endowus and S$736 at StashAway. Below about S$15,000 the fixed US$18 makes Endowus cheaper, and from S$250,000 Syfe’s 0.45% tier wins, then StashAway from about S$620,000. Fund costs aren’t included.

Can I invest CPF with AutoWealth?

Yes — CPF Ordinary Account savings, from S$3,000, at a 0.28% advisory fee, the lowest robo fee for CPF here (Endowus charges 0.40% plus GST). Fund costs of 0.32%–0.40% and your CPF agent bank’s charges of about S$2–S$2.50 per transaction and per quarter apply on top. Holdings sit in your name with Phillip Securities. AutoWealth also offers SRS portfolios.
